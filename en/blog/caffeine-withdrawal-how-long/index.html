How Long Does Caffeine Withdrawal Last and When Will You Finally Feel Better?

Yesterday, your last cup of coffee. Today, you've got a headache, you can't concentrate, and even the simplest tasks seem to require an unreasonable amount of effort. It feels like you barely slept, even though you got a full eight hours.

If coffee used to be part of your daily routine and you've suddenly stopped drinking it, you might be experiencing caffeine withdrawal. It's your body's response to reducing or stopping your usual caffeine intake.

How long does caffeine withdrawal last? Symptoms typically begin 12–24 hours after your last dose of caffeine, peak around 20–51 hours, and usually disappear within 2–9 days. However, everyone's experience is a little different.

Caffeine Withdrawal Symptoms: What to Expect

Not everyone experiences coffee withdrawal in the same way. Some people notice a little extra tiredness, while others find the first few days considerably more difficult.

Common caffeine withdrawal symptoms include:

  • Headaches. One of the most common symptoms of caffeine withdrawal. The pain may feel dull, pressing, or throbbing, and sometimes it can be strong enough to interfere with everyday activities.
  • Fatigue and sleepiness. You might feel like you barely have enough energy for simple tasks, even after a decent night's sleep.
  • Difficulty concentrating. Everyday tasks may take longer, and staying focused can feel unusually difficult.
  • Irritability and low mood. During the first few days, you may notice less patience, motivation, or enthusiasm than usual.
  • Nausea and muscle discomfort. These symptoms are less common, but they can also occur during caffeine withdrawal.

And remember, caffeine withdrawal isn't limited to coffee. It can also happen when you suddenly stop regularly consuming energy drinks, strong tea, or other sources of caffeine.

Why Do You Get a Headache After Quitting Coffee?

One of the most common questions about quitting coffee side effects is why stopping caffeine can cause such an uncomfortable headache.

Caffeine doesn't just affect alertness. It also influences blood vessels, including those in the brain.

Caffeine can temporarily narrow blood vessels in the brain. When you suddenly stop consuming it, changes in blood vessel activity and cerebral blood flow may contribute to withdrawal headaches.

Researchers consider this one possible explanation for caffeine withdrawal headaches, although the exact mechanisms are more complex.

Interestingly, caffeine is also an ingredient in certain headache medications. So the same substance that can help relieve pain in some situations may contribute to headaches when regular consumption suddenly stops.

If your headache begins shortly after quitting coffee, caffeine withdrawal could be the explanation. However, not every headache is caused by caffeine.

Caffeine Withdrawal Timeline: Day by Day

Although there's no exact schedule that applies to everyone, the caffeine withdrawal timeline generally follows a recognisable pattern.

Day 1: Your Body Notices the Change

Around 12–24 hours after your last caffeine dose, the first withdrawal symptoms may appear.

You might notice a mild headache, unusual tiredness, or a strong urge to take a nap. Tasks that normally feel easy may suddenly require more effort.

Day 2: Withdrawal Symptoms Reach Their Peak

For many people, this is the most uncomfortable stage.

Headaches may become stronger, fatigue can feel more noticeable, and concentrating on work or everyday responsibilities may be difficult.

If you feel worse on the second day than on the first, that doesn't necessarily mean something is going wrong. It's a common part of the caffeine withdrawal process.

Days 3–5: Things Start Getting Easier

For many people, symptoms gradually begin to improve.

Headaches may become less frequent or less intense, while energy levels and concentration start returning to normal.

The improvement isn't always immediate or consistent, but this is often the point when the worst of the withdrawal is behind you.

Days 6–9: Withdrawal Symptoms Usually Disappear

By this stage, most people have recovered from the main physical symptoms of caffeine withdrawal.

That doesn't mean everyone will suddenly feel fantastic on day nine. Some people recover much sooner, while others need a little more time.

How to Relieve Caffeine Withdrawal Symptoms

If you've already stopped drinking coffee, the most useful approach is to support your body while it adjusts, rather than trying to flush caffeine out of your system.

  • Make time for rest. Concentration and energy levels may be lower during the first few days. If possible, avoid scheduling your most demanding tasks during this period.
  • Stay hydrated. Drinking water won't directly cure caffeine withdrawal, but dehydration can contribute to headaches and fatigue.
  • Eat regularly. Long gaps between meals may make you feel worse, so try to maintain your usual eating routine.
  • Consider a slower reduction if symptoms become difficult to manage. There's no need to push through severe discomfort just to quit caffeine all at once.

If you miss having a warm drink as part of your daily routine, naturally caffeine-free teas such as rooibos or peppermint (herbal teas; check blends with tea or mate) can be comforting alternatives.

They won't eliminate withdrawal symptoms, but they can help you maintain a familiar ritual without adding more caffeine.

When Will You Feel Normal Again After Quitting Coffee?

For many people, the most uncomfortable part of caffeine withdrawal lasts only a few days, although those days can certainly feel longer.

The important thing to remember is that the headaches, tiredness, and difficulty concentrating during the first few days don't necessarily reflect how you'll feel living without caffeine in the long term.

Once the withdrawal symptoms fade, it becomes easier to understand how your body actually feels without its usual caffeine dose.

And if you're still planning to quit coffee, gradually reducing your intake may help you avoid some of the more uncomfortable symptoms.
