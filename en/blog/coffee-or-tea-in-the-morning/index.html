Coffee or Tea in the Morning? The Great Morning Debate

Contents

  2. Does Coffee Give You More Energy?
  3. What Is a Caffeine Crash, and Can Tea Help You Avoid It?
  4. What Are the Best Teas to Drink Instead of Coffee?
  5. So, Is Coffee or Tea Better in the Morning?

Some people start their day with the smell of freshly brewed coffee. Others reach for tea, while some can't imagine a morning without matcha. Everyone has their favorite ritual and a few strong opinions about why their choice is the best.

But which is actually better in the morning: coffee or tea? Does stronger coffee mean more energy? Or could tea help you avoid that dreaded energy slump a few hours later?

Coffee or tea in the morning? Both can help you feel more alert thanks to their caffeine content. Coffee generally contains more caffeine, while tea offers plenty of options with different caffeine levels. The best choice depends on your caffeine sensitivity and how you want to feel.

Coffee vs. Tea: Which Has More Caffeine?

An average 8 oz (240 ml) cup of brewed coffee contains around 80–100 mg of caffeine. The same amount of black tea typically has about 40–50 mg, while green tea often contains approximately 20–45 mg.

However, caffeine content varies depending on the type of coffee or tea, how much you use, the brewing method, and serving size.

For example, a single espresso may contain less caffeine than a large mug of brewed coffee, even though it tastes much stronger. A serving of matcha can also contain more caffeine than regular green tea, depending on how much powder you use.

So if you're trying to cut back on caffeine, counting milligrams is more useful than counting cups.

If you drink three cups of coffee containing around 95 mg of caffeine each, that's 285 mg per day. Replacing just one with a lower-caffeine tea can bring that total down.

Does Coffee Give You More Energy?

Caffeine doesn't actually provide energy the way food does. Instead, it temporarily blocks the effects of adenosine, a chemical in your brain that helps regulate sleepiness.

As a result, you feel less tired and more alert.

Because coffee usually contains more caffeine than tea, its effects can be stronger. But a higher dose doesn't necessarily mean you'll feel better.

For some people, strong coffee can cause jitters, anxiety, or a racing heartbeat. If you tolerate caffeine well, though, your morning coffee may be a perfectly good choice.

What Is a Caffeine Crash, and Can Tea Help You Avoid It?

You have your morning coffee, feel wide awake for a while, and then, a few hours later, the tiredness creeps back in. It's harder to concentrate, and another cup suddenly sounds very tempting.

That feeling is often called a caffeine crash.

As caffeine wears off, the tiredness it was masking can become more noticeable, especially if you haven't had enough sleep.

So does tea prevent this?

Not necessarily. Caffeine is the same molecule whether it comes from coffee, green tea, or matcha. There's not enough evidence to suggest that caffeine from tea is naturally absorbed much more slowly.

However, a typical cup of green tea contains less caffeine than coffee, so some people may notice less dramatic changes in alertness.

Green tea and matcha also contain L-theanine, an amino acid that has been studied in combination with caffeine. Research suggests this combination may improve certain aspects of attention, although it doesn't guarantee you'll avoid an energy crash.

What Are the Best Teas to Drink Instead of Coffee?

Switching from coffee to tea doesn't have to mean giving up caffeine completely. You can choose a drink based on the flavor and caffeine level you prefer.

  • Black tea – Rich and full-bodied, often a good choice if you want to cut back on caffeine without giving up your morning pick-me-up.
  • Green tea – Typically lower in caffeine than coffee, with a fresh, sometimes grassy flavor.
  • Matcha – A bold green tea made from finely ground tea leaves. It contains caffeine and L-theanine, and its caffeine content can be relatively high.
  • Oolong tea – A caffeinated tea with flavors ranging from floral and fruity to rich and roasted.
  • Pu-erh tea – Known for its distinctive, often earthy flavor, making it an interesting option for those who enjoy stronger-tasting drinks.
  • Yerba mate – A traditional South American drink made from the leaves of the yerba mate plant (Ilex paraguariensis). It has a grassy, slightly bitter taste and can contain substantial amounts of caffeine, making it a popular coffee alternative.

If you'd rather skip caffeine altogether, hibiscus or chamomile tea can be great options for a warm morning drink.

So, Is Coffee or Tea Better in the Morning?

There's no clear winner.

Coffee may suit you better if you love its flavor, tolerate caffeine well, and prefer a stronger boost in alertness.

Tea might be a better fit if you want to reduce your caffeine intake, explore new flavors, or find an alternative to your usual coffee.

If you often experience an unpleasant energy slump after coffee, try a smaller caffeine dose and see how you feel. You don't have to quit coffee entirely. Replacing just one cup is a good place to start.

Maybe some mornings you'll want a strong coffee, other days you'll reach for matcha, and on weekends you'll enjoy a cup of oolong.

In the end, it's not about what everyone else drinks. It's about what works for you. Your morning cup should be something you enjoy, not something you feel you can't start your day without.
