Matcha latte: recipe and caffeine

Whisk 2 g of matcha with ~60 ml of 80–90 °C water and add warm milk or a plant drink. A serving has 38–89 mg of caffeine (typical 63 mg): less than a cup of filter coffee (80–107 mg) but more than black tea (14–48 mg).

What you need

  • 2 g matcha (about half a teaspoon)
  • ~60 ml water at 80–90 °C
  • 150–200 ml milk or plant drink
  • Optional: sweetener

How to make it

  1. Sift the matcha to avoid lumps.
  2. Add hot (not boiling) water and whisk with a bamboo whisk or small frother until foamy.
  3. Warm the milk or plant drink and pour into the matcha.
  4. Sweeten to taste.

How much caffeine

You drink the leaf powder with matcha, so there is more caffeine than in an ordinary green tea infusion. In our data a serving (2 g, ~200 ml) has 38–89 mg (typical 63 mg), so size and timing matter if you pick it instead of coffee.

Tips

  • Hot but not boiling water is less bitter.
  • For the evening choose rooibos latte (no caffeine).
  • Matcha quality varies a lot, so count with the range, not the typical value.

FAQ

How much caffeine is in a matcha latte?

In our database a serving with 2 g matcha has 38–89 mg.

Is matcha stronger than coffee?

Usually not: a cup of filter coffee has 80–107 mg, a matcha serving 38–89 mg.
