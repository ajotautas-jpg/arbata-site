Sencha: caffeine, brewing and taste

Wikipedia gives an estimate of about 20–30 mg of caffeine per cup.

At a glance

Caffeine

In the measurements it cites, dry leaves hold roughly 27–31 mg/g, and a higher brewing temperature extracts more caffeine.

How it is made

Sencha is made from the upper leaves of Camellia sinensis. The leaves are steamed for 15–20 seconds to stop oxidation (Chinese green teas are pan-fired instead), then rolled into thin cylinders and dried. Sencha makes up about 80% of Japan's tea production.

Taste

The sources give no specific taste description. A general rule for green tea: water that is too hot or steeping that is too long makes it more bitter.

How to brew

About 80 °C water for about 1 minute.

Frequently asked questions

How much caffeine is in sencha?

Wikipedia gives an estimate of about 20–30 mg of caffeine per cup.

How do you brew sencha?

About 80 °C water for about 1 minute.

Why is sencha brewed with cooler water than black tea?

The sources give about 80 °C and about 1 minute. A higher temperature also extracts more caffeine, so cooler water suits people who want less.
