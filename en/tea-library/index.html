Tea library: types, caffeine and brewing

The type of tea decides more than flavour: it also shapes the caffeine. Here are the seven most common types, with ranges, brewing and sources.

From less to more caffeine

Types are ordered by average caffeine per cup. Ranges overlap, so a particular cup can differ.

  • Black tea: Usually fuller in flavour and darker than green tea; it is the most common tea in teabags.
  • Green tea: Usually lighter, fresher and grassier; water that is too hot or steeping that is too long makes it more bitter.
  • Oolong tea: A wide range, from light and floral to richer and roasted, depending on oxidation and roasting.
  • White tea: Lighter than most green or black teas, with a pale yellow cup.
  • Pu-erh tea: Young raw pu-erh is often strong, bitter and astringent; well-aged it turns smoother and earthy. Ripe pu-erh has a fermented, earthy taste.
  • Yerba mate: Like an infusion of vegetables, herbs and grass, somewhat like some green teas; often bitter when steeped in hot water.
  • Matcha: A strong umami flavour, which is mostly attributed to amino acids such as theanine.

Caffeine in tea and theanine

Tea also contains L-theanine. A review in Nutrition Reviews (2008) says tea taken at regular intervals may help maintain alertness and focused attention, and that theanine together with caffeine may improve attention switching. General information, not medical advice.
